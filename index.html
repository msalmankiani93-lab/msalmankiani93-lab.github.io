Materials physics · Photovoltaics · DFT

Research at the intersection of energy materials and optoelectronics.

I am a physicist and materials scientist working on solution-processed perovskite solar cells, printed and flexible optoelectronics, and energy materials. My work combines experimental fabrication and characterization with first-principles calculations.

Research profile

Areas of focus

Experimental and computational approaches to materials for energy applications.

Perovskite photovoltaics

Solution-processed solar cells, printed devices, flexible optoelectronics, and electron-transport layers.

Oxide photoelectrodes

Computational materials science

DFT studies of hydrides, oxides, inverse perovskites, and materials relevant to hydrogen storage.

Materials characterization

Structural, morphological, optical, electrical, and photovoltaic analysis of energy materials and devices.

From ink to device

A typical printed-device workflow

The exact materials and order depend on the cell architecture and processing conditions.

Prepare materials

Select ink composition, transport layers, substrate, and target architecture.

Prepare substrate

Clean and condition the substrate to support uniform coating and reliable interfaces.

Deposit layers

Apply functional films using solution processing, such as spin coating or slot-die coating.

Form the absorber

Control drying and film formation to obtain a continuous perovskite layer.

Complete and test

Finish the contacts, then assess structure, optoelectronic response, and device performance.
